Phase 3 — Time-Series Risk Evolution: How Fast is the Crisis Growing?

Mindset Unlock

The finale. Today's job is not just to count, but to predict. A 5% default rate that has stayed 5% for six months is stable. A 5% default rate that was 1% last month is a breakout. Velocity, the rate of change over time, is the most important metric for any executive. You are looking for the exact moment the EduFin portfolio lost its health.

CEO'S EXECUTIVE BRIEF — "When did this start, and how fast is it moving?"

The Board of Directors is meeting on Monday. They need to know the 'Genesis Month' of this default surge and the current 'Velocity' (month-over-month growth rate). Your final report must identify the trend, show the acceleration, and synthesize insights from all previous phases into one coherent narrative.

AI Thinking Assistant — Multi-Tool Suite

Use this final suite to synthesize your 3-phase investigation:

Antigravity (Strategy): "The Board wants a 'Crisis Forecast'. How can I use the LAG() function on 500k records to predict next month's total defaulted value? What is the mathematical risk of doubling?"

Databricks AI: "Help me write a query to calculate the 'Rolling 3-Month Default Rate' to smooth out seasonal noise."

STEP 3A — Open Metric: "Genesis Month" Discovery

What you're doing: The Board wants to know the EXACT moment we lost control. You must define what "Out of Control" means quantitatively (e.g., Velocity > 50%).

STEP 3B — Monthly Default Trend

What you're doing: Count how many loans defaulted each month. Look for the first month where defaults exceeded 50, then 100, then 200. This is the raw timeline of the crisis.
When did the count first double in a single month? That is your 'Trigger Point'.

STEP 3C — Default Velocity (Month-over-Month Growth)

What you're doing: Use LAG() or a self-join to compare this month's defaults to last month's defaults. Calculate the percentage growth. This 'Velocity' metric is what captures executive attention.
Is the velocity increasing, decreasing, or plateauing in the most recent two months? This determines if the crisis is 'out of control' or 'stabilizing'.

STEP 3D — Lag Time (Origination to Default)

What you're doing: Calculate the average number of days between the loan start date and the default date. This shows 'Survival Time'. If survival time is dropping, it means new loans are defaulting faster than old ones.
Note the lowest average survival time. (e.g., 'In August, loans were defaulting in just X days on average').

STEP 3E — Final Strategic Synthesis (Executive Dashboard)

What you're doing: Combine your core findings from all phases into a single, high-stakes table. This is the summary the Board of Directors will use to decide if they should fire the CEO or double down on collections.
Read your 'Analyst Notes' (status labels). Do they tell a clear story of deterioration across the phases?

BUILD YOUR CUSTOM COLUMNS — Required Submission

THE ADVANCED FINALE — Relative Trends & Prediction: You've mastered CASE, Windows, CTEs, and Correlated Subqueries. Now use relative growth logic inside your window functions. This is how you identify 'Crisis Acceleration'.

Column 1 — risk_acceleration: Compare last month's velocity with this month's velocity. Calculate the difference in percentage points. Label as 'Accelerating Crisis' (> 5% point increase), 'Stable Crisis' (constant velocity), or 'Slowing Crash'.

Column 2 — worst_path_projection: Calculate a simple linear projection for NEXT month's defaults. Formula: (this_month_defaults) * (1 + velocity_decimal). This is the 'Scare Metric' for the CEO.

THE FINAL WHY GATE — PROVE YOUR VALUE

1. Look at your 'Survival Days' Trend (origination to default). What is the trend telling you about the quality of NEW borrowers EduFin was acquiring in the later months? (Write 2-3 sentences).
2. Based on EVERYTHING you have seen over the last 3 phases (Customer segments, Income gaps, Institutional bias, and Time trends) — what is your ONE SINGLE RECOMMENDATION to the Board? "We must [Action] by [Date] because [Evidence]." One sentence. Make it powerful.
3. What was the 'Genesis Month'? Identify the exact month the crisis first surged and provide the supporting data (Volume issuance that month vs Default velocity 2 months later). Prove your detective work.

THE ENGINEER'S LENS: PHASE 3

LAG(def_rate, 1)
OVER (ORDER BY month) df.withColumn("prev_rate",
  lag("def_rate", 1).over(Window.orderBy("month")))

Window functions in Spark trigger a Global Sort. On 500k rows, if you don't define a PARTITION BY, all data is shoved onto a single CPU core to ensure strict temporal order. An engineer always summarizes data into monthly aggregates before applying window logic.

Note: Modern engineering stacks leverage 'Structured Streaming' to detect these trajectory shifts in milliseconds, moving away from batch-only reporting.
