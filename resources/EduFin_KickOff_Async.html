01 What This Is

This is a 5-day investigation.

You are not here to watch a tutorial and copy answers. You are here to work through a business problem, write queries, test assumptions, and explain what your analysis means.

You will move through three investigation files in order:

FIRST
PH
Portfolio Health
SECOND
CR
Customer Risk
THIRD
TA
Time Analysis

The goal is not just to get output. The goal is to show how you think, what you checked, what changed, and what your analysis points to.

02 Why This Feels Different

Three things to know before you begin. Not as disclaimers. As honest context.

REASON 01

No single right answer

Two people can query the same data and still reach different conclusions if their assumptions are different. What matters here is the quality of your reasoning.

REASON 02

Day 1 may feel unclear

That is normal. The full picture usually becomes clearer only after you start working through the data. Do not wait for perfect clarity before beginning.

REASON 03

AI is allowed, but unexplained work does not count

You may use AI tools. But if you submit a query, you must be able to explain why you wrote it, what it checks, and what you concluded from it.

Compare this to most SQL work: Kaggle dataset. Clean rows. One answer. GitHub. Done. That teaches tools. This investigation puts you inside a real business problem and asks you to move.

Your submission must include:

  • your reasoning
  • what you expected
  • what you found
  • what is still unclear

Submit through the form link only. Do not submit through WhatsApp. Do not submit through email. Do not send screenshots with no explanation and treat that as a submission.
Your work counts only after it is submitted through the form.

08 How To Work Well

Do not rush into random queries. A better approach:

1
Read the question carefully.
2
Identify what the business is really asking.
3
Decide what data points matter.
4
Write the query.
5
Check whether the result actually answers the question.
6
Note what changed in your thinking after you saw the result.
A query that runs is not automatically a good answer.

09 Rules and Certification

AI Usage

Use it. But own every line.

Use any AI tool you want. But if you submit a query, you must be able to explain it. If you cannot explain it, the submission does not count.

Ambiguity

Unclear is intentional.

Some parts will not have one clean answer. That is intentional. This work is about judgment, not just matching output.

Communication

Silence is the fastest way to fall behind.

If you are blocked, say what you tried, where it failed, and what is unclear.

Work Order

Follow the sequence.

PH first. CR next. TA after that. Skipping the order weakens your submission and your certificate consideration.

What counts toward certificate consideration

  • Required submissions completed
  • Reasoning included in every submission
  • Active participation across the investigation
  • Following the work order
  • Showing genuine effort

What weakens certificate consideration

  • Disappearing without context
  • Submitting only output with no reasoning
  • Skipping the work order
  • Copying work you cannot explain
This is not a course completion badge. It reflects whether you worked through a real investigation with seriousness.

11 LinkedIn and Portfolio (Optional)

This part is optional. If you want to document your work publicly, keep it focused on your thinking — not on the data.

You may share: what you were trying to understand, what you assumed first, what changed after checking the data, and one safe lesson from the investigation.

Safe to share
  • Your reasoning and what shifted
  • A lesson about scale, joins, or business logic
  • A workflow snapshot that does not expose private information
Do not share
  • Raw row-level data or private identifiers
  • Sensitive business numbers
  • Full answers or notebook exports publicly
  • Public GitHub repos for these materials
If you keep notes or notebooks for your portfolio, keep them private and focus on your thinking, not on exposing the underlying data.
