# EduFin Portfolio Risk Analysis — Presentation Script
*Estimated delivery time: 3–4 minutes*

---

Good afternoon, everyone. My name is Siddhartha, and today I'm going to walk you through a crisis investigation I conducted for EduFin Capital — an education loan company facing a serious financial and compliance emergency.

---

**Setting the Scene**

EduFin discovered a ₹12 Crore discrepancy in loan disbursements, affecting over 15,000 student accounts in just six months. With a federal compliance audit approaching, the CEO needed board-ready answers within 48 hours. I was brought in as the Data Analyst to investigate the portfolio and deliver actionable insights. I structured my analysis into four sequential phases — each one zooming in closer, from the big picture all the way down to individual names and institutions.

---

**Phase 1 — Portfolio Health Check**

The first question was simple: how big is this portfolio, and how much have we already lost?

EduFin has 5,000 loans across 3,000 customers, with a total portfolio value of ₹204.82 Crores and an average loan size of ₹4.1 Lakhs. Here's where the crisis becomes clear — 590 loans have defaulted, representing an 11.80% default rate. The industry benchmark for education loans sits between 5 and 8 percent. EduFin is nearly double the upper limit. In rupee terms, ₹24.24 Crores is already lost. On top of that, another 273 loans worth ₹10.94 Crores are overdue and at imminent risk of defaulting. Altogether, ₹35.18 Crores — 17.26% of the entire portfolio — is at risk. I classified the portfolio health as HIGH RISK, and the 3.4% loan closure rate confirmed this isn't a temporary market shock. It's a systemic origination failure. My recommendation: activate crisis protocols immediately.

---

**Phase 2 — Geographic Risk Analysis**

Once we knew the scale, the next question was: which cities are bleeding the most money?

I built an Exposure Score for every city — a composite of default rate and portfolio size — to rank them by actual capital loss. Eight out of fifteen cities were classified as Critical. The worst three: Pune had the highest default rate at 20.13% with ₹1.14 Crores in losses. Ranchi had the highest overall exposure score — a 17.86% default rate and ₹1.40 Crores lost. Lucknow had the largest portfolio at risk, with ₹7.51 Crores deployed and a 13.97% default rate. My recommendation was a tiered lending halt — immediately in Ranchi, Pune, and Lucknow; within 30 days in Indore, Jaipur, Ahmedabad, and Kochi; and enhanced monitoring for Delhi and Nagpur. This isn't a blanket policy — it requires a region-specific response.

---

**Phase 3 — Customer Risk Segmentation**

Phase 3 shifted the question from where to who. The Collections team needed a prioritized action list, not just a summary.

I built a Priority Score for every defaulting customer by combining defaulted amount, number of defaults, and CIBIL score. The top finding was one Critical-tier customer — Raghav Dubey — with 3 defaults, ₹15.07 Lakhs in losses, and a CIBIL score of just 486. This is a serial defaulter who needs immediate legal escalation and recovery agency involvement. Below him, 12 High-risk customers collectively owe over ₹1.16 Crores, each with 100% default rates. Every customer in the output was mapped to a specific collection action — from aggressive daily calls for High-risk borrowers to full legal recovery for Critical ones. What I handed over wasn't just a report — it was a daily call list ready to act on.

---

**Phase 4 — Institutional Partnership Risk**

The final and hardest question: which partner institutions are consistently sending EduFin students who default?

I scored every institution using a Health Score and classified them into four tiers — Blacklist, Monitor, Retain, and Reward. Thirteen institutions were flagged for blacklisting, all with default rates above 15%. The worst was State Commerce Institute at 20.87% default rate and ₹83.96 Crores in defaulted exposure — that alone justifies immediate contract suspension. Notably, five of the top seven blacklist candidates were Institutes, pointing to a systemic quality issue with that institution type. But there was a critical positive outlier: National Law Institute had a 0% default rate despite handling over 10,000 loans. My recommendation to the Partnership Director was — before suspending the 13 bad partners, study what National Law Institute is doing differently in student screening, and replicate that model across the portfolio.

---

**Closing**

To bring it all together — across four phases, I moved from a macro health check showing an 11.80% default rate, to geographic hotspots losing over ₹1 Crore each, to individual borrower targeting with actionable priority scores, and finally to institutional accountability with 13 partners flagged for blacklisting.

Every phase answered a different question. Phase 1 told us *how bad* it is. Phase 2 told us *where*. Phase 3 told us *who*. And Phase 4 told us *why*. Together, they give EduFin everything it needs — not just to survive the audit, but to rebuild a healthier portfolio going forward.

Thank you.
